For a long time, the Riemann hypothesis was considered the holy grail of mathematics. It’s a super important problem, and generations of mathematicians tried and failed to prove it. And then last month, OpenAI landed a hit with a weaker version of the hypothesis. And on Friday, Levent Alpöge and Claude landed the knockout.

And that means one thing. The final boss of mathematics is now P versus NP. Until Monday, that boss was basically untouched. Then Anthropic and OpenAI both released proofs of VP ≠ VNP, the arithmetic cousin of P versus NP.

So what does that mean? And how close are we to solving P versus NP? I talked about it with Amir Shpilka, one of the leading experts on this question.

Ok cut. VP!=VNP happened on Monday, we did the video on Tuesday. Now it’s wednesday, and the video is already out of date. So here’s the short version of what happened today. 

The background is — P vs NP problem is so important because it’s about proving that some problems don’t have fast algorithms. You should think about it as a milestone - if we can solve it, we should hopefully be able to pinpoint the complexity of million other problems. 

Now VP vs VNP is like P vs NP but for arithmetic problems — you should think about matrix multiplication, Fourier transform, this kind of stuff. And guess what. VP vs VNP was solved and today, openai announced new results that pinpoint the arithmetic complexity of matrix multiplication, Fourier transform, and bunch of other stuff. 

So what we hoped to happen with normal algorithms just happened with arithmetic algorithms — we solved this abstract problem that’s like P vs NP and suddenly we are cooking and we can understand all the concrete important problems we really care about. 

Another insane thing — a few days ago, the vibe was that AI can solve problems but can’t develop theories. Well, guess what — not only GPT developed a theory, the theory solves the biggest questions of an entire field that was considered hopelessly hard. VP vs VNP is The kind of stuff we never expected to understand in our lifetimes.
